## 3. Discussion

### 3.1 Landscape document

The chair presented proposed fields for a shared map of current work on agent identity across the ecosystem. Fields include name, type, link, description, out of scope, status, and who added the entry. An ARIA entry was shown as a worked example, including its known boundaries.

Esteban Garcia identified several existing ecosystem initiatives across NIST, OpenID, W3C, IETF, MCP, and Stanford, and suggested broadening the document's scope to encompass identity in general rather than AI agent identity specifically. The group will discuss this in the next meeting before the document opens for entries.

The landscape document fields will be circulated to the community for feedback.

### 3.2 Demo — The Bunny and the Carrot

The chair presented a working demo of ARIA credential registration and scope authorization at [thebunny.bar](https://thebunny.bar) (credential issuer) and [thecarrot.rest](https://thecarrot.rest) (credential receiver). The demo features credentials with a 3-minute validity window; expired credentials are refused by the receiver.

Esteban Garcia and Ivan Moreno discussed credential expiration handling and the separation of authentication from authorization. Esteban Garcia also asked about integration with DIDs and transport protocols such as IPEX.

Markus Sabadello suggested extending the demo with protocol verification levels — allowing visitors to see the difference between trust levels in practice.

Aaron Grego noted the demo illustrates both credential registration and ATP implementation.

### 3.3 Protocol specification and governance

Markus Sabadello asked whether the group's output would serve as the official specification deliverable. The chair clarified that community discussions inform the ARIA specification, which remains with TrustLayer Foundation. The group's role is to advise, not to own the specification.
