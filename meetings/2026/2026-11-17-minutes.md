## 2. ARIA and agent.bar updates

The chair reported that agent.bar, the first issuer built on ARIA, is live for testing. L0 is for testing and email confirmation only. L1 requires an identity document, a liveness check and proof of control of a domain in the DNS. Organization accounts can then upgrade to L2 and L3, where the platform simulates only the organization review and the validation of the organizational role or of the person's binding to the organization.

The Trust Ledger records the lifecycle of credentials. Ivan Moreno explained that the ARIA AP SDK lets the receiver issue a signed receipt of each request, so that sender and receiver can each keep a record of the interaction, whether or not the agent was admitted. A scope change means the credential is revoked and a new one is issued. TrustLayer Foundation and agent.bar do not keep transaction records. Signed receipts can be verified offline with the evidence each party retains, but this does not prove past authorization states.

## 3. Hiring use case

Prasanna Bableshwar outlined a hiring flow with sourcing, screening, scheduling and background check agents. The test considered was whether independent auditors can reconstruct an agent's historical authority after credentials are revoked.

## 4. Open question

Prasanna Bableshwar asked how it can be known that registry information persists over time, that is, how a third party can trust that the append-only Trust Ledger has not been altered. No settled answer was given. The question is also recorded in the comments on the v0.2 document.
