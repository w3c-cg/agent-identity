## 3. Discussion

### 3.1 ARIA Protocol — presentation

The chair presented the ARIA Protocol to the new participants, covering the accountability framing, the trust level architecture (L0–L3), the Agent Trust Protocol, and the Foundation's governance structure.

Several points were raised during the presentation. Four topics were raised that the group will want to return to.

**DNS agnosticism.** Esteban Garcia asked whether L1's requirement for domain control could be replaced by other anchoring mechanisms, naming identity wallets as one alternative. The question was whether the spec should remain agnostic to the anchoring technology rather than prescribing DNS.

**C2PA alignment.** Esteban Garcia proposed exploring whether ARIA credentials could compose with C2PA identity assertion models, which bind signatures to organizational identity. C2PA is backed by major industry participants and represents a concrete path to interoperability with existing document and content provenance infrastructure.

**Authorization scope and organizational heterogeneity.** Esteban Garcia raised the practical challenge of translating authorization scope across organizations with different internal structures, jurisdictions, and delegation models — questioning whether a single universal schema is achievable.

**Authentication versus authorization.** Esteban Garcia asked whether agent authentication between organizations is in scope. The chair clarified that the specification covers authorization, not authentication; the authentication layer is intentionally left to other mechanisms. Aaron Grego noted that the protocol is designed to remain compatible with multiple distinct authentication approaches.

## 4. Written topics and suggestions received

The following topics and suggestions arrived before or around the meeting date and are noted for the record. They were not discussed on the call.

**Evgenii Arsentev** filed issue #2 in the repository, raising the question of sub-agent identity: if a substantial share of model calls in an agentic run come from sub-agents, what does an identity asserted at the top of the run actually cover? The spec currently has no sub-agent issuance; every credential carries `delegationDepth: 0`.

**Nicholas Templeman** submitted a written note to the list on identifier collisions that fail open: two distinct subjects can canonicalise to the same form, so the check that should discriminate cannot. He asks what properties a registry must bind alongside an identifier to make a false match impossible rather than merely unlikely. 

**Parviz Shariff** shared the Authority Resolution Framework (ARF, arXiv:2608.15832) as a potential input document, covering authority provenance across five domains with a JSON-LD representation. He offered to file a detailed mapping to the group's work.

The group will assess scope alignment before taking them up as agenda items.
